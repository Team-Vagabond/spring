01 · The problem

In a Darchula village, the spring the whole ward drinks from has given a quarter less water for two years — and nobody who could act knows why.

Finding out means a hydrogeologist, a GIS analyst, rainfall records from Kathmandu, and a two-day trek. For this spring, that has never happened. It just slowly goes dry.

02 · Who, exactly

Ranju Bhatta

WASH focal person at a rural municipality office.

  • One desk. Part-time on water. No GIS, no hydrogeology.
  • Responsible for 50–250 springs she cannot visit.
  • Hears about a failing spring months late, from a complaint — with no way to triage which of the 250 is worst.

She is not "farmers in Nepal". She is one findable desk, in every one of Nepal's 753 local governments.

cron ─▶ sweep DeepSeek-V4-Flash · 1 call/sensor · pure-code anomaly maths │ spring crosses threshold ▼ BOUNDED AGENT LOOP MAX_STEPS 12 · token+NPR meter · structured trace goal in ─▶ model picks the next tool check_sensor · check_flow_history · check_rainfall→Open-Meteo map_recharge_area→AWS SRTM+D8 · compare_satellite→Copernicus Sentinel-2 note_hypothesis (memory) · request_dispatch ─▶ ★ HUMAN GATE ★ routing: Flash for recon → gpt-5.5 once evidence competes · visible failure+retry │ awaiting_approval ▼ HUMAN approve │ reject │ request more ─▶ file case + send Nepali SMS
  • Goal, not a script — two runs of one case, two different tool orders.
  • Memory — hypothesis beliefs across the run; per-spring history across runs, in Postgres.
  • Routing — 90% of calls on the cheap model; we can show the cost gap.

06 · The bad day

The week the rain does not stop. No usable satellite. Sensor underwater and offline. LLM key rate-limited. Rainfall four days stale.

  • Sweep still runs — cheap model, deterministic fallback
  • Investigation routes entirely to the fallback model
  • Offline sensor → status UNKNOWN, not "failed"
  • Satellite evidence dropped · stale rainfall flagged
  • Can't separate rain from a broken pipe remotely → low-confidence case: "send someone to look"
  • Nepali SMS still drafts — and queues for approval

Simulate a bad day — runs exactly this. The handback to a human is the design.

07 · What it takes to be real
Cost per runSweep of 6 springs: NPR 0.03. An escalated investigation: ~NPR 7. A full day for a 200-spring municipality: ~NPR 100 · USD 0.75.
Who paysThe municipality's WASH budget, or a WASH-sector programme (ICIMOD / HELVETAS / SNV / Red Cross already fund springshed work by hand). This replaces the evidence-gathering, not the hydrogeologist.
Route to usersNo app store. It rides the municipal water desk and the District Water Resources Committee — institutions that already exist and already meet. The SMS reaches a handset the coordinator already carries.
At 10,000 springsSweep is linear and cheap; bottleneck is LLM quota → more to the fast model, batch, cache. Sentinel Hub free tier ≈ 100–300 investigations/month, then ~USD 0.30 each.
Next 3 monthsOne 10-spring sensor pilot with one municipality · one hydrogeologist-verified springshed to benchmark the estimate · gauge/CHIRPS rainfall · IVR for coordinators who don't read comfortably.
