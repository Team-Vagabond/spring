Naula / नौला — a spring source

Our agent helps a rural municipality's water desk in Darchula work out why a mountain spring is quietly failing and what to do — without sending a hydrogeologist to every spring, or waiting months for anyone to notice.

The user, named

Ranju Bhatta, the WASH focal person at a rural municipality office — one person, part-time on water, no GIS or hydrogeology training, responsible for 50–250 springs she cannot possibly all visit. Today a village complains, months after the decline started; she has no way to triage or to arrive at a spring already knowing what changed around it.

What the agent does, unasked

A cron sweep checks every sensor against its own seasonal baseline. The moment one shows a sustained decline, an agent — with no human in the loop — investigates: rainfall, the recharge area traced from an elevation model, a then-vs-now satellite comparison of that area, competing hypotheses weighed and re-weighed. It then drafts a case and a Nepali SMS brief and stops for a coordinator to approve.

Tools & models

Models: DeepSeek-V4-Flash for the sweep + recon (≈90% of calls), gpt-5.5 for the judgement once hypotheses compete. A full 6-spring daily cycle costs ~NPR 14; a quiet day ~NPR 0.03.

External tools: Open-Meteo (ERA5-Land rainfall), AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM DEM), Copernicus Sentinel Hub (Sentinel-2 L2A), Supabase Postgres, a simulated SMS gateway.

Human checkpoint

The agent may never file a case or send a message itself. request_dispatch drafts them and the loop halts (awaiting_approval). Filing and sending happen only via an explicit human approve. Loop capped at 12 steps; nothing is ever left running. Shown blocking, live, in the demo.

The bad day

The week the rain does not stop. Ten days of cloud — Sentinel-2 returns nothing usable. The worst-hit spring's sensor has been underwater for two days and is offline. The shared LLM key is rate-limited by fifteen teams. Open-Meteo's grid is four days behind. Naula does not bluff: the sweep still runs on the cheap model; the investigation routes fully to the fallback model, treats the offline sensor as status UNKNOWN (not a failure), flags stale rainfall, records that satellite is unavailable, and — unable to separate rainfall deficit from abstraction or damage without a field check — drafts a low-confidence case whose recommendation is "send someone to look". The Nepali SMS still drafts and queues for approval. Every degradation is written into the trace. Simulate a bad day runs exactly this.
