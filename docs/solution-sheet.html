Mul / मूल — a spring source

Mul helps a rural municipality's water desk in Darchula work out why a mountain spring is quietly failing and what to do — without sending a hydrogeologist to every spring, or waiting months for anyone to notice.

The user, named

Ranju Bhatta — the WASH focal person at a rural municipality (gaunpalika) office. One desk, part-time on water, no GIS or hydrogeology training, responsible for 50–250 springs she cannot possibly all visit. She hears about a failing spring from a village complaint, months late, with no way to triage which spring is worst or to arrive already knowing what changed around it. There is a desk like Ranju's in every one of Nepal's 753 local governments.

What the agent does, unasked

A cron sweep checks every sensor against its own seasonal baseline. The moment one shows a sustained decline, an agent — with no human in the loop — investigates: sensor health, flow history, rainfall, the recharge area traced from an elevation model, a then-vs-now satellite comparison, six competing causes weighed and re-weighed. It drafts a field report and stops for someone at the water desk to accept it.

Architecture

Monitoring sweep
A cron job hits /api/cron. Every sensor is checked on DeepSeek-V4-Flash — one call each — against pure-code anomaly maths (seasonal baseline, z-score, year-on-year).

Tools & models

Models: DeepSeek-V4-Flash for the sweep and recon (~90% of calls), gpt-5.5 for the judgement once hypotheses compete. A real Khalanga investigation cost NPR 5.42; a quiet daily sweep of seven springs, ~NPR 0.03.

External tools: Open-Meteo (ERA5-Land rainfall, 25 yr), AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM DEM), Copernicus Sentinel Hub (Sentinel-2 L2A), Supabase Postgres.

Human checkpoint

The agent may never file a case itself. request_dispatch drafts it and the loop halts (awaiting_approval). Filing happens only on an explicit human accept — one button, at the bottom of the report, after the reader has read the case. Loop capped at 12 steps; nothing is left running. Shown blocking, live, in the demo.

The bad day — the week the rain does not stop

Ten days of cloud — Sentinel-2 returns nothing usable. The worst-hit spring's sensor has been underwater for two days and is offline. The frontier model is timing out under load and Open-Meteo's grid is four days behind. Mul does not bluff: the sweep still runs on the cheap model; the investigation routes fully to the fallback model, treats the offline sensor as status UNKNOWN (not a failure), flags stale rainfall, records that satellite is unavailable, and — unable to separate rainfall deficit from abstraction or damage without a field check — drafts a low-confidence case that says "send someone to look", not a confident wrong cause. Every degradation is written into the trace. The degraded path (?degraded=1) runs exactly this.
